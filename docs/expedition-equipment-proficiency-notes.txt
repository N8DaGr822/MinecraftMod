Further assistant suggestions, not settled requirements:

- Berserker could improve native attack recharge rather than bypass it.

Suggested activation rules still need design approval and balancing:
- All four worn pieces share the style; inactive loadout armor grants nothing.
- Iron and diamond pieces may mix if they share the same style.
- Material retains base stats; do not multiply the style bonus by material tier.
- Changing loadouts cannot reset cooldowns or carry a counter window forward.
- Define supported spears/axes, what counts as a timed block, stacking limits,
  multiplayer authority, HUD feedback, and removal behavior before implementation.
- Consider separating visual appearance from combat specialization later, so
  players are not forced into a particular appearance to get their preferred buff.

No numeric bonuses, cooldowns, combat hooks, skill requirements, or implementation
milestone are approved here. This remains separate from the planned unified
temperature pass. Current visual additions: War Chief (commander), Knight,
Berserker, and Samurai for iron and diamond, using cosmetic smithing variants.

DESIGN GOAL
Make exploration harder through destination-specific preparation, equipment, and
environmental hazards. Support family multiplayer with different play schedules:
regular players earn useful proficiency advantages, while returning players can
be equipped by the group and join adventures without grinding usage requirements.
Taking a few days away should not prevent someone from exploring with the group.

AGREED DIRECTION

1. Portal destinations and late-game threats
- The user is already adding dimensions and working on a pirate-themed portal.
  Treat that as existing work to inspect, not a request to recreate it here.
- New mobs and encounters can belong to other destinations reached through
  portals that players must open.
- A Lost Cities / Parasites-inspired destination is a strong fit: dangerous
  ruined environments and creatures can form a distinct expedition experience.
- Exact portal requirements, dimension layout, rewards, and progression placement
  remain undecided. Do not assume the pirate destination contains the parasites.

2. Temperature and expedition preparation
- Temperature is a desired feature, making different areas harder to explore.
- Extreme areas should require specialized preparation. Vanilla equipment alone
  should not provide a satisfying sustainable answer to the hardest climates.
- Add environmental equipment and enchantments such as warmth and cooling.
- Enchantments should make exposure more manageable, but must not solve every
  temperature challenge or eliminate the role of specialized armor.
- Design toward ordinary gear for mild conditions, enchanted ordinary gear for
  limited harsh exposure, specialized gear for sustained regional exploration,
  and specialized gear plus provisions/enchantments for extreme expeditions.
  Exact boundaries, protection values, and exposure times require balancing.

3. Better armor appearance and meaningful armor families
- Improve the basic armor visuals toward recognizable, substantial armor.
- Environmental specialization is a reason to add armor sets with different
  purposes, rather than simply increasing defense on a linear equipment ladder.
- The user's example: wear cold-protective equipment in the mountains, then
  switch inside a suitable cave to spelunking equipment emphasizing light and
  fall protection, where less cold protection is needed.
- Weapons are a lower priority. Keep the existing legendary-item direction;
  do not turn this discussion into approval for a large new weapon catalog.

4. Three equipment loadouts with craftable upgrades
- Provide three selectable armor loadouts in the inventory/menu so spare gear
  sets do not consume ordinary inventory space.
- Begin with one loadout. Craftable upgrades unlock the second and third.
- The user explicitly selected craftable upgrades so frequent players can help
  brothers or other returning players catch up and participate.
- Follow the existing inventory-expansion approach where applicable; inspect its
  implementation before designing storage, unlocks, or menu behavior.
- Recipes, costs, acquisition timing, switching rules, and exact slot coverage
  remain to be specified. Accessories are not automatically approved by this.

5. Activity-based proficiency, with no usage gates
- Improve proficiency slowly by performing the relevant activity.
- Improvements provide bonuses, never requirements for using equipment/tools
  or participating in exploration.
- User examples: combat increases damage; mining improves gathering.
- Regular players retain a rewarding advantage without making less-frequent
  players unable to contribute when supplied with appropriate equipment.
- Skill categories, gain rates, caps, bonus amounts, and persistence details
  remain design work. Eight skills and two separate leveling systems are not
  requirements; the original mod references were inspiration.

PROPOSALS DISCUSSED, NOT FINAL REQUIREMENTS

Temperature model
- Biome/dimension establish climate; altitude, time, exposure, and wetness modify
  it. Shelter, clothing, meals, drinks, and the proposed bonfire provide options.
- Exposure accumulates with clear warnings before serious penalties.
- Armor changes alter warming/cooling rates rather than instantly resetting
  body temperature. Cave temperature depends on actual conditions: a shallow
  ice cave can remain cold even when deeper sheltered passages are milder.
- Start with temperature without a constant thirst meter.
- Keep food choices meaningful: a warming meal should have alternatives rather
  than becoming the only usable meal in an entire region.

Armor and enchantment concepts
- Mountaineering: fur-lined layers, reinforced boots, insulation, wind protection,
  and possibly traction.
- Spelunking: compact reinforced armor, helmet light, impact/fall protection,
  and possible climbing utility.
- Desert: wrapped cloth, hood/face covering, lighter segmented plates, cooling.
- Maritime/wet weather: treated leather, overlapping scales, resistance to
  soaking and its cold effects; possible connection to pirate content.
- Containment: enclosed helmet and sealed-looking joints, with protection against
  particular parasite hazards if those hazards are selected.
- Model direction: breastplates, shaped shoulders, gauntlets, knee guards, and
  substantial boots within Minecraft proportions. Prototype helmet/chest visuals.
- Candidate enchantments: Insulation (less heat loss), Cooling (shed heat), and
  Weatherproofing (less soaking or shorter wetness). Names and mechanics are open.
- Avoid one universal set; insulation may be a disadvantage in hot conditions.

Loadout behavior
- Store actual items with durability/enchantments; only the active set grants
  effects. Keep weapons/hotbar independent initially.
- Permanent unlocks survive death; all stored equipment follows normal death and
  keepInventory rules. These were recommended defaults, not finalized rules.
- Consider a short switch duration, interruption on damage, and preventing
  mid-fall switching to stop last-moment defensive swaps.
- Define item conservation, full-inventory handling, death drops, multiplayer
  synchronization, save compatibility, and duplicate attribute/effect prevention.

Proficiency behavior
- Possible tracks: combat, mining, farming, cooking, exploration, animal handling.
- Possible benefits: modest damage/handling, gathering yield/efficiency, crop
  yield, meal duration, endurance/recovery, and mount endurance/recovery.
- Reward productive actions; prevent placed-block loops and harmless-target
  farming from becoming optimal progression. Specify legitimate repeat activities.
- Keep bonuses bounded, with slower high-level gains and clear progress feedback.
- Define mining interactions with Fortune, Silk Touch, and placed resources.
- Recommended defaults: proficiency survives death and does not decay offline.
- Environmental proficiency should improve efficiency/recovery, not become a
  hidden prerequisite for surviving a destination with appropriate equipment.
- Consider destination/player-action difficulty rather than escalation merely
  because the multiplayer server has been running while someone was absent.

Ruined-city expedition concepts
- Portal-opening objective, dependable return route, and an arrival foothold.
- Explore districts, recover resources, and encounter increasingly dangerous nests.
- Opening sealed areas or disturbing hives can escalate local threats.
- Rewards support armor, enchantments, and a few legendary items.
- Initially contain infestation within its destination. Overworld spread would
  require a separate explicit gameplay decision.

OTHER INITIAL IDEAS AND THEIR DISPOSITION
- First Aid: independent lethal head/torso pools were raised initially, but not
  selected. They conflict with existing health/heart assumptions and need separate
  design. Nonlethal injuries, bandages, splints, and camp recovery were alternatives.
- Simple Difficulty: temperature selected; thirst/dehydration not selected.
- Reskillable / Level Up! Reloaded: adapt toward one activity-based improvement
  system. Hard equipment gates are explicitly rejected.
- Lycanites Mobs / Ice and Fire: creature roles, taming, mounts, dragons, and lairs
  remain inspiration. No wholesale roster or new dragon system was approved.
  Inspect the home Griffon and existing bosses, including Ice Wyrm, before overlap.
- Spartan Weaponry: broad weapon expansion is lower priority per the user.
- Baubles / Quality Tools: accessory slots, utility relics, and material-funded
  refinement were discussed, but are not selected requirements. Chosen refinements
  rather than random penalty rerolls were a suggestion only.
- Lost Cities / Parasites: desired inspiration for a dangerous portal destination.
- Treat named mods as design references for this document. Actual dependency
  integration versus original mechanics remains unconfirmed; no compatibility,
  installation, licensing, or porting decision is implied.
